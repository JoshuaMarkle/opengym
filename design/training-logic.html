1What this document is

This is the working spec for OpenGym's training logic: how the app decides what to suggest for the next session, how it judges whether a lift is going up, holding or going down, and how it projects goals. It's written to be tuned first, then implemented as pure functions in src/lib/training/. The app screens are in design/proposals/progressive-overload.html. This document covers the rules behind them.

Every example table here is generated from a reference implementation of the rules, so the tables and the rules can't disagree. When a number changes during tuning, the tables change with it. The constants are collected in §24.

Revision 2: suggestions are now worked out set by set with a fit zone (§6) instead of one up / stay / down call for the whole exercise. The old 60% set 1 weighting, back-off rule and −10% down rule are gone: the fit zone produces all three on its own. New in this revision: the effort target asked at setup (§4), Suggest and Auto modes (§9), "break or missed logs?" on comebacks (§11) and exercise order (§12).
Status: draft for tuning. Nothing here is approved yet. SPEC §1 currently lists goals and +weight hints as out of scope for v1.

2Principles

  1. Every set should land in the rep range, at the effort the person chose. The range (6–8, 10–12) comes from the routine. The effort target (default RIR 0–2) comes from setup. A set that does both is a good set and its weight stays.
  2. Each set is judged on its own. Strength drops across sets, so one weight for every set often can't keep them all in range. Every set gets its own estimate and its own next weight.
  3. Set 1 leads, the rest match where they can. Set 1 always gets its own best weight. Sets 2+ then share a weight with set 1 or with each other when that still keeps them in range. Ideally two of three sets match.
  4. Suggestions assume you'll repeat last time. The estimate is built from what you did, at the effort you logged. It never assumes you'll get stronger before the next session.
  5. Never change a box without the person. In Suggest mode nothing changes until ✓. In Auto mode, LBS arrives pre-filled and the person can overwrite it. Reps are never touched.
  6. Breaks are always a question. A long gap could be a real break or missed logs. The app asks and never auto-fills after a gap.
  7. Every lift is judged against itself. A lateral raise and a deadlift don't move at the same speed.
  8. Say "not sure" rather than guess. Sparse, gappy or scattered data gets no status or projection, plus a plain explanation.
  9. Ask almost nothing, never nag. One effort question at setup. No notifications. A dismissed suggestion doesn't come back until the numbers change.

5Steps: how big is one move?

There's no per-exercise step setting. The step comes from the equipment and is then learned from what the person actually logs.

Learning the step. After 4 or more sessions, the step is the most common positive difference between consecutive working weights for that exercise, if it appears at least twice. For example, a machine logged at 90, 100, 100, 110 learns 10. A person who always jumps dumbbells 25 → 30 → 35 keeps 5. The learned step is never used to suggest a weight the person has never logged, unless it's exactly one learned step away.

Big steps. On light dumbbells the next step can be a big percentage jump (20 → 25 lb is +25%). The fit zone can then be narrower than one step, so no step-aligned weight lands in range. Rule: a set that topped out (reps ≥ repsMax at or under the target effort) always goes up one step, and the row is flagged "big step" when the formula predicts fewer reps than repsMin. That honours your 20 × 10 @R0 → 25 example. Whether to keep it is open question 1.

6The fit zone: one estimate per set

Every working set from the last session gives a strength estimate. From that estimate the app can predict, for any weight, how many reps the person would get at any effort. The fit zone is the band of weights where that prediction lands inside the rep range at the target effort.

E(set)        = lbs × (1 + (reps + RIR) / 30)                 // Epley, with RIR added to the reps
reps(W, rir)  = 30 × (E / W − 1) − rir                        // predicted reps at weight W, leaving rir in reserve
W(reps, rir)  = E / (1 + (reps + rir) / 30)                   // the weight for a given reps and RIR

zone.lightest = W(repsMax, targetMax)    // top of range with the most reps left
zone.heaviest = W(repsMin, targetMin)    // bottom of range at the hardest effort

The shaded area is where the curves for the target RIRs cross the rep range. The ring is what was logged, the diamond is next time's weight, and the bar on the x-axis is the zone in lbs.

The third dimension: RIR

Stack one row per RIR and the zone becomes a 3D shape (lbs × reps × RIR). The easiest way to read it is in slices: each row is the band of weights that keeps reps in range at that RIR, and the target rows are the bright ones.

Set 1 above (200 × 13 @R0, E ≈ 287). The white line is the logged 200. At RIR 0 the band is 205–226, so 200 is too light to reach failure inside the range. At RIR 2, 200 is right on the edge. The union of the target rows (R0–2) is the zone: 196–226.
Accuracy. Epley is good from about 3 to 12 reps and pessimistic above ~15. With high rep ranges (15–20) or very easy sets (reps + RIR > 20), the estimate is rough. Later, the constant 30 can be learned per person and per lift from sets at different weights (open question 2).

7The decision, set by set

Each working set is decided on its own first, then the weights are matched.

1Topped out → up. reps ≥ repsMax and RIR ≤ targetMax: the person can't add reps, so the weight has to move. Next = one step up, or more if the zone is further up (well past the range). Flagged "big step" when the predicted reps at the new weight fall under repsMin.
2In the zone → keep. reps in range and RIR inside the target. Weight stays. If RIR was above the target minimum, a green rep hint asks for one more rep.
3Otherwise → move to the estimate. Next = W(middle of the range, middle of the target), rounded to the step and kept inside the zone. That's up when the set was easy or past the range, down when it was under the range at failure, and often the same weight when it was short with reps left (§8 #9).
4Match. Set 1 keeps its own weight. Then try, in order of preference: all sets at set 1's weight · set 1 alone, sets 2+ sharing one weight · sets 1 to n−1 at set 1's weight, the last set alone · every set separate. A shared weight must be a step-aligned weight inside every member's zone, and later sets are never heavier. Pick the lowest cost: distance from each set's own weight (in steps) + 3 per extra distinct weight.
5Aim. For every set whose weight changed, the expected reps at the new weight and the middle target RIR, clamped to the range. Shown as green text in Previous (Suggest) or as "aim" (Auto).
// src/lib/training/decide.ts — mirrors decide_fit() in the reference implementation
export function decide(sets: WorkingSet[], range, step, target, lastSetOnly): Suggestion {
  const targets = targetsFor(sets.length, target, lastSetOnly)
  const fits = sets.map((s, i) => fitSet(s, range, step, targets[i]))     // E, zone, ideal, kind: 'top'|'keep'|'move'
  const own = fits.map(f => f.ideal)
  const next = bestPattern(own, fits, step, MATCH_PENALTY)                // set 1 anchored, later sets never heavier
  return { perSet: next.map((w, i) => ({ weight: w, move: cmp(w, sets[i].weight), aim: aimFor(fits[i], w, targets[i]) })), why }
}

When the bar shows. If any set's weight changes, the exercise gets one bar listing the next weights ("Next: 205 · 205 · 180"). It's green when set 1 goes up or stays, and orange when set 1 goes down. If every set keeps its weight, there's no bar, only rep hints. The decision is computed when the exercise appears in a workout, from the last session of that exercise (not the routine). If today has more sets than last time, the extra sets copy the last set's weight. If it has fewer, the extra ones are ignored.

What replaced what. The old back-off rule (set 1 past, later sets collapse) is now just rule 3 on sets 2+. The old "down after 2 sessions" and "−10% on barbell" are now the estimate (rule 3), which jumps straight to the weight that fits instead of guessing a percentage.

Break or missed logs? Replaces the bar on the first exercise after a gap.

A gap in the log can mean two things: a real break, or training that wasn't logged. The app can't tell, so it asks once per lift on the first session after the gap. It never auto-fills after a gap, even in Auto mode.

  • I took a break: the comeback ramp below.
  • I trained, just didn't log: normal suggestions from the last session. The gap is marked "not logged" in charts and isn't treated as a break by trends.
  • No answer (✕ or ignored): no suggestion today for that lift. Whatever was lifted is scored next time as normal.

When every lift has the same gap, the question is asked once on Home for all of them.

A gap of 21+ days since the last session of that lift is a break. The first session back gets an orange bar suggesting (once the person says it was a break) 90% of the last working weight (80% after 42+ days), rounded down to the step. The next 3 sessions ramp linearly back to the old weight. During the ramp, the normal rules are paused for that lift, but any set that falls under the range at failure gets its normal estimate. Ramp sessions are left out of trends.

12Exercise order and fatigue (later)

Doing preacher curls before hammer curls makes the hammer curls weaker, and the other way round. The fit zone assumes today looks like last time, so a swapped order gives a suggestion that's too heavy or too light. This section is a design for later, not v1 of training.

What it needs

  • Muscles per exercise. The library has one muscle. Add secondary: Muscle[] to seed exercises (not shown or asked in the UI; custom exercises get none).
  • Overlap between two exercises: same primary = 1.0 · one's primary is the other's secondary = 0.5 · secondary with secondary = 0.25 · else 0.
  • Pre-fatigue of an exercise in a session = Σ over earlier working sets in that session of overlap × effort, where effort = max(0.4, 1 − 0.15 × RIR). Three hard preacher sets (RIR 1, 0, 0) before hammer curls (overlap 0.5) = 1.43.

The adjustment

E_fresh  = E_logged / (1 − k × preFatigue_then)       // put every past session on the same footing
E_today  = E_fresh × (1 − k × preFatigue_today)        // today's planned order (routine order, updated live as exercises are done)
k        = prior 0.04 per unit, capped so the total adjustment is ≤ 25%
           learned per exercise once it has ≥ 3 sessions both fresh (preFatigue = 0) and pre-fatigued:
           k = (1 − median E_after / median E_fresh) / median preFatigue

Example: hammer curls (range 8–12, target R0–2), alternating order.

SESSIONORDERPRE-FATIGUESET 1 LOGGEDe1RM LOGGEDe1RM, FRESH-EQUIVALENT

Oct 4After preacher (3 sets)1.4340 × 9R052.055.9
Oct 8Hammer first0.0040 × 12R157.357.3
Oct 11After preacher (3 sets)1.4340 × 9R153.357.3
Oct 15Hammer first0.0045 × 9R160.060.0

Learned k = 0.049 per unit, so 3 preacher sets first cost about 7% of hammer curl strength. With 5 lb dumbbells both orders round to 40 lb today, but the aim changes: first → ~12 reps (close to topping out, so 45 is next), after preacher → ~9 reps. Without the adjustment, a session after preacher curls would read as a bad day and could pull the weight down.

When order changes mid-workout (the person starts hammer curls first when the routine says preacher first), the suggestion is recomputed when the exercise is opened. Trends and goals use E_fresh, so order changes don't look like strength changes.

Machines and fatigue cost. Machines that use the same muscles get overlap from their primary and secondary muscles in the same way. A per-machine "how tiring" factor isn't needed: the effort term already counts how hard the sets were.

13One strength number per session

Trends, pace, projections and milestones all use one number per session per lift: the effort-adjusted est. 1RM of set 1.

e1RM = weight × (1 + (reps + RIR) / 30)          // Epley with reps-in-reserve added
// RIR missing → assume 0 (same as the fit zone). Sets with reps + RIR > 15 are skipped (Epley is poor past ~12–15).
// Bodyweight: the series is set 1 reps. Assisted: the (negative) weight. BW+added: the added weight's e1RM.
// Exercise order (§12, later): use E_fresh so a swapped order doesn't look like a strength change.

Why set 1 and not the best set? Set 1 is the least fatigued, so it moves with real strength and not with rest times or set counts. Records (SPEC §4) keep using the best set, because records are about the best thing you did. If set 1 is skipped (reps + RIR > 15), the session uses the next working set that qualifies.

15Trend status: climbing, holding, slipping

Every lift (focus lifts only, when Train everything is off) gets a status after each session.

window   = last 56 days of set-1 e1RM, comeback sessions removed
require  ≥ 6 sessions spanning ≥ 28 days, otherwise NOT SURE YET

wobble   = 1.4826 × median |residual| ÷ median e1RM            // robust noise, %

SLIPPING   change ≤ −max(3.0%, 1.5 × wobble)  and  ≥ 3 of the last 5 sessions below the window median
STEADY     otherwise, on a slow lift (pace < 0.5%/mo)
HOLDING    otherwise
// hysteresis: a new status must come out of 2 evaluations in a row before it's shown

18Goals and projection

A goal is weight × reps on one lift, with an optional date. It converts to a target e1RM with RIR 0: target = w × (1 + reps/30). 225 × 5 → 262.5.

The curve

Gains slow down, so a projection never uses a straight line. The current rate r₀ (lb/week, from the trend fit blended with pace) decays with time constant τ (from §14):

expected(t) = E₀ + r₀ · τ · (1 − e^(−t/τ))         // t in weeks from now, E₀ = current trend value
fast(t)     = same with r₀ × 1.5 and τ × 1.3
slow(t)     = same with r₀ × 0.5 and τ × 0.7
ceiling     = E₀ + r₀ · τ                         // where this lift is heading at the current rate
Band between the slow and fast curves. Dashed white line: 225 × 5 = 262.5.
Why not a straight line: the first months' rate would overshoot badly.

If at least 9 months of history exist, τ is refit from the lift's own data: τ = Δt / ln(r_early / r_recent), clamped to 0.5–2× the prior.

expected(date) ≥ goal  → LIKELY
fast(date) ≥ goal      → STRETCH
otherwise              → UNLIKELY

On track or behind

When the goal is created, the app stores startE1rm and the expected curve at that moment. The "should be here" tick is that curve evaluated today. The goal shows ahead when the trend value is at or above the tick, and behind after 2 weeks in a row below it while the recomputed verdict isn't LIKELY. Behind offers: move the date to the new LIKELY date, lower the target to what's LIKELY by the date, or keep it. Goals set with your own rate (C3) use that rate's curve for the tick.

Done = any logged set with weight ≥ target weight and reps ≥ target reps. Est. 1RM passing the target only marks it READY.

21Train everything and focus lifts

  • Train everything (on by default): every lift with enough data gets a status, milestones and goals. Most people want to be stronger everywhere.
  • Focus lifts (up to 5, the existing pinnedExerciseIds): listed first in Progress, the only lifts that can produce Home "Lately" lines, and the default candidates for Month in review. They're pre-filled from the 4 most-logged compound lifts after the 6th workout.
  • Train everything off: statuses, milestones and goals only for focus lifts. Every other lift collapses to one row (records and charts). Workout suggestions still run for every lift either way.

Home "Lately": at most 2 lines, from focus lifts and goals. Priority: goal done > goal behind (once) > status change to CLIMBING or SLIPPING (shown once). Milestones never prompt here; you make one a goal if you want prompts. Nothing when nothing changed this week.

26Open questions

  1. Big step on topped-out sets. 20 × 10 @R0 → 25 goes up, but Epley predicts ~2 reps at 25. Keep "topped out always goes up", or keep the weight and add reps until the zone reaches the next step?
  2. Learn the formula. Epley's 30 is a population average. Learn it per person (and maybe per lift) from sets at different weights, once there's enough data?
  3. One bad set 1. Set 1 one rep short at failure drops weight straight away (§8 #16). Should set 1 need two sessions in a row before it goes down, like v1?
  4. Matching cost. A penalty of 3 steps per extra weight makes set 2 go up to match set 1 in #10. Higher means more matching, lower means more sets on their own weights.
  5. Per-exercise effort target. Some people go to failure on isolation lifts but not on squats. Worth a per-exercise override, or does "last set only" cover it?
  6. Exercise order. Worth building, and is a hidden secondary-muscle list in the seed library acceptable?
  7. Auto mode edits. When someone overwrites Auto weights often on one lift, should that lift switch to Suggest by itself, or only offer it?
