Progressive overload, goals and strength trends

How the app helps you get stronger without crowding the screen. During a workout the hints are green text in Previous, and a bar only shows up when the app thinks you should change weight. Everything else lives in Progress, with your focus lifts first, and only speaks up when it has something to say.

ASSUMPTIONS ABOUT THE AVERAGE LIFTER
1Trains hard, inside a rep range. The range (6–8, 8–12) is the ideal, effort (RPE or RIR) is logged as a badge on reps, and failure inside the range is good. Set 1 matters most, because later sets drop off.
2Wants to be told the next number, not taught the theory. One bar, one number, a Why for the curious, and always a way to say no.
3Could be any level. Some people are new, and some arrive with 10 years of training. Pace comes from their own history (or an import, or one setup question), never from a fixed beginner rate.
4Wants to get stronger everywhere, but cares about a few lifts most. Train everything is on by default, with focus lifts first. People who skip legs can switch it off.
5Logs imperfectly. Missed sessions, forgotten weeks and holidays happen. Gaps are blank, not zero, and the app says "not sure yet" instead of guessing.
6Doesn't want to configure anything. Six settings in total (F6), all with defaults. Anything smarter is inferred.
Revision 3, after review: effort is a badge on reps (no RPE column), and suggestions never touch REPS. Up / stay / down weigh set 1 at 60%, with a new back-off case (A7) and a set 1 tip (A9). Why sheet buttons match Prev / Next. Goals can have no date or a custom date, the projection is live while picking, and messy data gets a choose-your-pace scale instead of a projection (C3). F · PACE, FOCUS AND MESSY DATA

Different lifts, different lifters, imperfect logs

Answers to the review concerns, with as few new options as possible. Lifts move at different speeds, so each one is judged against its own pace. People care about some lifts, so trends only run on focus lifts. Logs have gaps, so gaps are blank, not drops. Lifters arrive with years of training, so the first weeks start from an import or one question. And progress slows down over time, so projections follow a curve.

E · THE MATH

How it decides, in plain rules

All on-device, all from data the app already stores. Every rule runs per exercise. Thresholds are starting points to tune with real logs.

RULEINPUTHOW
Set scoring (A3, A8)Last session, rep range, effortEach working set is past (above repsMax), in (inside the range), short (1 under repsMin) or well short (2+ under). The rep range is the ideal, and hitting failure inside it is the goal, not a problem. Set 1 counts 60%, and the remaining working sets share 40%.
Go up (A1)Set scoresEvery set in range (or past) → +1 step, with the green bar. Set 1 past range at RPE ≤ 8 → +2 steps. Example: 20 × 10 @10 on all three sets of 10–12 → up.
Stay (A5)Set scoresSet 1 in range, later sets short → stay. No bar. Sets that came up short get a green "try" with the bottom of the range (or last reps + 1). Sets already in range get no hint.
Go down (A6)Set scores, 2 sessionsSet 1 well short at RPE ≥ 9.5 in 2 sessions in a row → orange bar, −1 step (about −10% on barbell lifts). Failure inside the range never triggers it.
Back-off (A7)Set scoresSet 1 past range at failure but sets 2+ well short → keep set 1, drop only sets 2+ by about 5% so they land in range. Orange bar, "try" on those sets only.
Set 1 tip (A9)Set scores over timeShown in the first 3 workouts, and again if set 1 is regularly below set 2 (often rushed warm-ups). One ✕ dismisses it for good.
DismissPast ✕ taps✕ hides the bar for this workout. After 2 dismissals of the same kind on an exercise, it waits until the numbers change before suggesting again.
Step size (F6)EquipmentBarbell: weightStep. Dumbbells: the next pair. Machines and cables: one stack plate. When a step is more than 10% of the weight, progress is counted in reps at that weight instead.
Own pace (F2)All history of the lift, import, experience answerFit e1RM(t) = A − B·e^(−t/τ) per lift (gains that slow down over time), giving the last 12 weeks more weight. Until there are 8 weeks of data, start from a typical curve for the lift's type and the experience answer.
Status (D1)Every lift (or focus lifts only), last 8 weeks, ≥ 6 sessionsCompare the 8-week trend with the lift's own pace and its own day-to-day wobble. CLIMBING clearly above zero. HOLDING flat for ≥ 4 weeks on a lift that usually moves. STEADY flat on a slow lift. SLIPPING down more than the wobble, with 3 of the last 5 sessions under trend. Fewer sessions → "Not sure yet".
Gaps (F3)Session datesThe trend is fitted on sessions, so missing weeks carry no weight. A gap of 21+ days counts as a break: those sessions are dropped from the trend and the comeback ramp starts.
Projection and verdict (C1, C2)The lift's curveExtend the fitted curve to the goal date. Band = the curve with faster and slower τ, widened by wobble. LIKELY the curve reaches the goal. STRETCH only the fast edge does. UNLIKELY neither does.
When not to project (C3)Last 12 weeksNo projection if there are fewer than 6 sessions, if the fit explains little of the variation (R² < 0.3), or if the slope's range includes both gaining and losing. Then the user picks a rate. Scale labels come from the lift's own pace: slow < 0.5×, typical 0.5–1.5×, focused 1.5–3×, rare > 3×.
No-date estimate (C7)Current trendDate at which the curve reaches the target. If the trend is flat or negative, or the estimate is more than 2 years out, show "not moving toward it yet" with no date.
