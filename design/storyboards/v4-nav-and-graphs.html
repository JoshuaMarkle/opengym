PART 2

What lifters actually want to see

The core question is "am I getting stronger?" The hard part is that you rarely lift the same weight for the same reps twice. 185 × 8 one week and 190 × 6 the next: which is better? The best graphs answer that without making you do the math.

#GRAPHWHY IT MATTERSPRIORITY
1Estimated 1RM trend, per liftConverts any weight × reps into one strength number (e.g. 185 × 8 ≈ 234), so sessions with different rep counts can be compared. It's the most direct "am I stronger" line.Must have
2Rep PRs (best weight at each rep count)Shows progress even when your 1RM is flat: "first time doing 185 for 8". It's the most motivating thing for lifters who train in the 5–12 rep range.Must have
3Top set per session (real weight)The actual heaviest weight and reps, not an estimate. People trust real numbers, and it's what you remember lifting.Must have
4Lift summary: change over a periodOne screen with every main lift and "+12 lb in 3 months". The fastest answer to "am I making progress overall".Must have
5This workout vs last timeImmediate feedback on the finish screen: which exercises went up, held or went down. This is where most motivation comes from, day to day.High
6Weekly sets per muscle groupThe main training-amount measure for muscle growth. It answers "am I doing enough chest?" rather than "am I stronger".High
7All lifts, % change since startPuts every lift on one scale so you can see which ones are moving and which have stalled.Nice to have
8Total volume (lbs × reps)Popular but noisy: it goes up just from adding sets. Better as a secondary option than a headline.Nice to have
9Bodyweight + strength relative to bodyweightMatters if you're cutting or bulking: "bench 1.2× bodyweight". Needs bodyweight entries.Nice to have
10Consistency (workouts per week, calendar)Doesn't show strength, but strength follows consistency. A small calendar or streak is enough.Nice to have

DECIDE

Decisions to make

● = my suggestion. Everything else is a real option.

1Which estimated-1RM formula?
  • Epley: weight × (1 + reps/30). Simple and the most common.
  • Brzycki: a bit lower at high reps.
  • Average of several formulas.
Ignore sets over 12 reps for the estimate either way; it gets inaccurate.
2Which set represents a session?
  • The set with the highest estimated 1RM.
  • The heaviest weight lifted.
  • The first working set.
3Do drop sets and warm-ups count?
  • Warm-ups never count. Drop sets count toward volume and sets per muscle, but not toward the 1RM estimate or records.
  • Drops count everywhere.
  • Drops count nowhere.
4Variations: separate or combined?
  • Separate history for each variation, with the "Flat ▾" menu to switch, plus an option to view them combined.
  • Always separate.
  • Always combined as one "Bench" line.
5Where does the Progress tab open?
  • G4 lift summary cards.
  • The last exercise you viewed (G1).
  • G7 all lifts, % change.
6Which lifts appear on the summary?
  • You pin them (default: your 4–6 most frequent).
  • Automatically, the most frequent.
  • Every exercise you've logged.
7Default time range, and what's on the x-axis?
  • 3 months, spaced by date (gaps show missed weeks).
  • 6 months.
  • Spaced by session (no gaps).
8Raw points or a smoothed line?
  • Raw dots plus a light trend line through them.
  • Raw line only (shows every bad day).
  • Trend line only.
9How is the change shown?
  • Both: "▲ 24 lb (11%)".
  • lb only.
  • % only.
10Do records come back anywhere?
  • No badge in the table (as you asked). Show records on G3, as dots on G1, and as a count on G5.
  • Only on G3.
  • Not at all.
11How to handle bodyweight exercises (dips, pull-ups)?
  • Chart reps until you add a bodyweight entry, then bodyweight + added weight.
  • Always reps only.
  • Ask for bodyweight at the start of each workout.
12Show goals on the charts?
  • Yes: a dashed line at the goal weight, with a "% there" label.
  • No; goals live on the Today screen only.
